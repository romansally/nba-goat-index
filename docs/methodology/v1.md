# GOAT Index Scoring Methodology — v1

**Status:** Authoritative for `method_version = "v1"`.
**Config:** `config/scoring_v1.yaml` is the single authoritative source of `method_version` and
all tunable values. The engine echoes `method_version` verbatim into every output, plus
`git_sha` when available.
**Change policy:** Edits to this file are allowed only for typos/clarity. Any change to defined
scoring behavior requires `v2.md`, an ADR, and a `method_version` bump (CLAUDE.md Rule 1).
**Decision provenance:** Component set, two-layer split, always-on era adjustment, config-driven
weights, scope toggle, and consensus policy are locked by the Vision doc (§3–§6). The nine
parameter decisions below were locked in the T2 interrogation session (2026-07-05) and are
documented individually in §12.

---

## 1. Philosophy: the two-layer split

There is no ground-truth GOAT. This method earns credibility through **method integrity**, not
false objectivity:

- **Objective layer (reproducible):** real inputs, per-possession + pace normalization,
  era-relative standardization, and six decomposed component scores. Same input → same output,
  always. Every score traces back to its components.
- **Weighting layer (explicit human judgment):** how the six components combine into one number.
  The weights live in `config/scoring_v1.yaml`, are documented below, and are the *only* place
  preference enters. **Custom Mode** is the same engine with different weights.

Era adjustment is **always on**. There is no toggle.

**The iron rule: if a result looks wrong, fix the method, not the player.** Players are never
hand-placed into rankings. Ever.

---

## 2. Notation and conventions

- **Season key:** the end-year of the season (1961-62 → `1962`). All era boundaries below use
  this convention.
- **Player-season grain:** one row per player per season, with regular-season and playoff stat
  splits, per `data/seed/player_seasons.csv`.
- **League reference table** (`data/seed/league_seasons.csv`, acquired in T3): per season —
  `pace` (league possessions per 48 min per team; hand-assembled values for pre-1978 seasons,
  flagged `pace_estimated`), league per-team per-game `PTS`, `TRB`, `AST`, league `TS%`,
  `season_games` (modal team schedule length — 82 normally, 50 in 1999, 66 in 2012, etc.), and
  `asg_held` (no All-Star Game was held in 1999).
- **The pool (P):** the 15–30 seed players. All min–max scaling is across this pool (see §12.2:
  scores are pool-relative by design).
- **Rounding:** the engine computes in full float64. Published CSVs round to 2 decimal places
  (half away from zero). The hand worksheet uses 4-decimal intermediates; golden tests compare
  final scores at ±0.01 tolerance.

---

## 3. Era adjustment (the objective layer's normalization)

Every counting stat is converted to an **era-relative ratio** in two steps:

**Step 1 — per-75 possessions.** For player-season *i* and stat *s*:

```
player_possessions_i = MP_i × league_pace(season_i) / 48
per75_s = total_s / player_possessions_i × 75
```

League pace is used for all players in all eras (uniform rule — team-level pace is unavailable
before 1973-74, and switching inputs by era is exactly the distortion this method avoids).
Team-pace refinement is a v2 candidate.

**Step 2 — divide by the league baseline.** The league per-75 baseline *per player-slot* (5 on
the floor) for stat *s* in a season:

```
baseline_s = (league_team_per_game_s / league_pace × 75) / 5
REL_s = per75_s / baseline_s
```

`REL_s = 1.0` means league-average production per possession-slot *in that season's environment*
— comparable across every era. Wilt's 1962 (50.4 ppg at pace ~125) and Jordan's 1988 (35.0 ppg
at pace ~91) land within ~0.2 of each other on `REL_pts`, which is the entire point.

League rebound baselines (`lg_trb_pg`) represent player-attributed rebounds only, excluding
team bookkeeping rebounds, uniform across all eras. This definition results from building all
league baselines on LeagueLeaders aggregates, since LeagueDashTeamStats serves no seasons
before 1996-97 (source details in `docs/sources.md`).

Note: this is **league-mean ratio normalization**, not the literal z-scores worded in Vision §3
— the substitution is deliberate and documented in §12.1.

**True Shooting** is already a rate; its era adjustment is a direct ratio:

```
TS = PTS / (2 × (FGA + 0.44 × FTA))
REL_TS = TS / league_TS(season)
```

## 4. The Season Production Index (SPI)

One number for "how much era-relative production did this season contain, per possession":

```
SPI = 0.50 × REL_pts + 0.25 × REL_trb + 0.25 × REL_ast
```

Points, rebounds, and assists are tracked in **every era** covered by the seed (rebounds from
1951; all seed careers start 1957+). Steals, blocks, and turnovers are excluded from v1 scoring
*by design* — they exist only from 1974/1978, and v1's uniform-inputs rule forbids feeding
different stats per era (they remain in the seed with era-conditional nulls for the contracts to
enforce; see §12.4 for the same reasoning applied to advanced metrics). The 50/25/25 blend is a
documented judgment call (scoring is the dominant production signal; rebounds and assists split
the rest evenly) and lives in config as an objective-layer parameter.

Playoff seasons get the identical treatment (`P_SPI`) using playoff totals and the same
regular-season league baselines (league playoff baselines are small noisy samples; the
approximation affects all eras alike — documented limitation, §12.10).

**Availability factor:** `AVAIL = min(1, GP / season_games)`. Because `season_games` is the
season's actual schedule length, lockout- and COVID-shortened seasons are not penalized.

## 5. The six components

Every component states: inputs, era availability, formula (raw value), and how it scales to a
0–100 component score (§6). Components use only all-era-available inputs — **no on/off, no
plus-minus, no era-switching inputs anywhere.**

### 5.1 Peak — value of the best N seasons (default N = 5)

- **Inputs:** season SPI; AVAIL. Era availability: all eras.
- **Qualifying season:** `AVAIL ≥ 0.5` — the player appeared in at least half his team's
  schedule that season. A 20-game hot streak cannot fake a peak season, and because the
  threshold scales with schedule length, lockout/COVID seasons qualify normally. If a player
  somehow has zero qualifying seasons, all seasons become eligible (deterministic fallback,
  flagged in run metadata).
- **Raw:** mean SPI of the top-`peak_n` qualifying seasons, ranked by SPI (ties: earlier season
  first). Players with fewer than `peak_n` qualifying seasons use all of them (Peak measures
  quality, not volume — a short-career high peak still registers; volume is Longevity's job).
- **Scaling:** min–max across the pool → 0–100.
- Why N = 5 and not 3 or 7: §12.5.

### 5.2 Longevity — sustained career value

- **Inputs:** season SPI; AVAIL. All eras.
- **Raw:** `Σ over all seasons of (AVAIL × SPI)` — the career volume of era-relative production.
  Decline years still add (SPI is a non-negative ratio); missed games and missed seasons simply
  add nothing. Active players accumulate "so far" honestly — no projection.
- **Scaling:** min–max across the pool → 0–100.

### 5.3 Winning / Impact — all-era proxy (no on/off, by construction)

On/off and plus-minus exist only from ~1996-97, so this component is built from two signals
available in every era (per Vision §5's candidate list):

- **Individual half — career Win Shares per 48:**
  `WS48 = career_total_WS / career_total_MP × 48`. WS is computable for all eras (league
  average ≈ .100 by construction, so it is era-stable). Season-level WS is a seed column
  (hand-assembled from documented published sources).
- **Team half — games-weighted career team SRS:**
  `SRS_w = Σ(GP_season × team_SRS_season) / Σ GP_season`. SRS (point differential adjusted for
  schedule) exists for all eras. Traded seasons store the GP-weighted SRS across teams.
- **Raw/scaling:** each half is min–maxed across the pool, then
  `Winning/Impact = 0.50 × MM(WS48) + 0.50 × MM(SRS_w)`.
- The 50/50 sub-weight is a fixed v1 choice with documented reasoning: §12.3. WS appears *only*
  here (not in Efficiency) to avoid double-counting.

### 5.4 Playoff — production × depth

- **Inputs:** playoff totals per player-season (GP, MP, PTS, TRB, AST). All eras.
- **Raw:** `Σ over playoff runs of (P_SPI × playoff_GP)` — quality times volume, so deep runs
  and sustained playoff excellence both count. Missed postseasons contribute 0.
- **Scaling:** min–max across the pool → 0–100.
- Rings and series wins are deliberately **excluded** from this component: §12.6.

### 5.5 Accolades — per-eligible-season rates (era-aware)

Awards did not all exist in all eras. v1 neither punishes players for awards that didn't exist
nor credits hypothetical wins:

- **Award types and introduction seasons** (end-year convention): MVP 1956 · championship
  ("ring") 1947 · Finals MVP 1969 · All-NBA 1947 · DPOY 1983 · All-Star 1951 (seasons with no
  ASG held, i.e. 1999, are excluded from All-Star eligibility counts).
- **Per-award rate:** `rate_a = weighted_wins_a / eligible_seasons_a`, where
  `eligible_seasons_a` = career seasons in which award *a* existed. All-NBA uses weighted
  selections: 1st = 1.0, 2nd = 0.5, 3rd = 0.25 (3rd team exists only from 1989 — documented
  approximation, §12.10).
- **Scaling:** each award's rate is min–maxed across the players with ≥ 1 eligible season for
  it. The component is the weighted mean over the player's eligible award types, with award
  weights renormalized over eligible types:

```
accolade_weights (sum = 1.0): MVP .30 · ring .25 · Finals MVP .15 · All-NBA .15
                              · DPOY .075 · All-Star .075
Accolades = Σ_eligible( w_a × MM(rate_a) ) / Σ_eligible( w_a )
```

Awards are **season-attributed** in `data/seed/accolades.csv` (required for peak scope, §7) and
must join a played player-season; the one real selection that cannot is excluded (§12.10).
This design is the direct answer to questions.md's era-fairness concern — worked Bill Russell
example in §12.7.

### 5.6 Efficiency / Advanced + Counting — era-safe set

BPM/VORP exist only from 1974, PER is patchy pre-1974, and WS is spoken for by Winning/Impact.
v1 uses two signals computable identically in every era (PER/BPM/VORP deferred to v2 — reasoning
in §12.4):

- **Efficiency half:** career `REL_TS`, minutes-weighted:
  `REL_TS_career = Σ(MP_s × REL_TS_s) / Σ MP_s`.
- **Counting half:** career average production quality, minutes-weighted:
  `SPI_career = Σ(MP_s × SPI_s) / Σ MP_s`. (Distinct lens from Peak — best-5 *mean* — and
  Longevity — availability-weighted career *sum*: this is the career per-possession *average*.)
- **Raw/scaling:** `Efficiency = 0.50 × MM(REL_TS_career) + 0.50 × MM(SPI_career)`.

## 6. Scaling and combination: min–max + weighted sum

**Min–max rule.** For raw value `x` across the pool:
`MM(x) = 100 × (x − min_pool) / (max_pool − min_pool)`.
Degenerate case (**award-rate elements**): if `max_pool == min_pool`, every player scores 50.0
on that element. An exact pool-wide tie is a legitimate outcome only for the discrete per-award
rates (e.g. every eligible player winning All-Star every eligible season, as in the fixture
trio); the continuous component inputs cannot legitimately tie pool-wide, so a zero or
noise-thin range there is **invalid input** under the §9 input-domain rule, not a scoring case
(decision record: ADR-0002).

Peak, Longevity, and Playoff are `MM` of a single raw value. Winning/Impact, Efficiency, and
Accolades are convex blends of `MM`-scaled sub-scores (sub-weights sum to 1.0) and are **not
re-stretched** after blending — they stay in [0,100] by construction but need not span it.

**Final score** (chosen over end-scaled z-sums for hand-computability and directly readable
component breakdowns — pool-relativity consequence documented in §12.2):

```
GOAT = Σ_components ( weight_c × Component_c )      ∈ [0, 100]
```

**Ranking ties** (determinism): sort by final score desc, then Peak component desc, then
`player_id` asc.

**Pairwise comparison** is a derived view of the same numbers — for players A vs B under the
current scope and weights: verdict = higher final score; the "why" = the component-by-component
table with the higher side highlighted. No separate math.

## 7. Scope toggle: career (default) vs peak

Locked by Vision §6; full semantics and the engine-reuse rationale in §12.9:

- **Career scope (default):** everything above, over the full career.
- **Peak scope:** the whole engine re-runs with each player's input restricted to his top-5
  qualifying seasons (the Peak window). Winning/Impact, Playoff, Efficiency, and Accolades are
  recomputed over that window only (awards are season-attributed; playoff runs belong to the
  window's seasons; award-eligibility counts use window seasons). **Longevity is dropped** and
  its 0.10 weight is redistributed proportionally across the other five components
  (each becomes `w_c / (1 − w_longevity)`; renormalized weights sum to exactly 1.0). All
  min–max scaling re-runs within the pool under peak scope.

## 8. The weighting layer: default weights and Custom Mode

Default weights translate the priority order recorded in Vision §5 — Peak → Winning/Impact →
Playoff → Accolades → Advanced+Counting, Longevity as its own added axis — into first-pass
numbers (provenance and adjustability: §12.8):

| Component        | Weight |
|------------------|--------|
| Peak             | 0.25   |
| Winning/Impact   | 0.20   |
| Playoff          | 0.18   |
| Accolades        | 0.15   |
| Efficiency       | 0.12   |
| Longevity        | 0.10   |
| **Sum**          | **1.00** |

- The `weights:` block in `config/scoring_v1.yaml` is the **Custom Mode surface**: swap the
  numbers (they must sum to exactly 1.0 — the engine errors rather than silently renormalizing)
  and the same engine re-ranks.
- Every other config value (`peak_n`, blends, accolade weights, intro seasons) is an
  **objective-layer parameter**: changing one is a behavior-changing scoring change and requires
  the full v2 path (new methodology doc + ADR + `method_version` bump + golden regeneration).

## 9. Missing data and input-domain policy

- v1 scoring inputs (GP, MP, PTS, TRB, AST, FGA, FTA, WS, team SRS, season-attributed accolades)
  are all-era-available **by design** — there is no imputation anywhere in v1.
- Nulls are **never** coerced to zero. Era-conditional nulls in the seed (3P before 1980,
  steals/blocks before 1974, turnovers before 1978) are enforced by the Pandera contracts and
  are simply not scoring inputs in v1.
- If a required scoring input is null, the engine **raises** — fail loudly, never impute.
- **Anchor-range input domain (ADR-0002):** a continuous component input (the Peak, Longevity,
  and Playoff raws; WS48; games-weighted SRS; career REL_TS and SPI) whose pool range is zero
  or within floating-point noise of zero — `max − min ≤ 1e-12 + 1e-9 × max(|min|, |max|)` — is
  **invalid input**: real careers never produce bit-identical or noise-thin pools, and min–max
  scaling would otherwise stretch that noise into rankings. The engine raises; it never scores
  it. Award rates keep the §6 degenerate rule for exact ties and are refused only when unequal
  yet within the same noise threshold. In peak scope the dropped Longevity input is exempt (it
  carries no weight and does not appear in output). This changes the accepted input domain
  without changing any score for an input that remains accepted — a behavior-affecting change
  shipping bump-free under CLAUDE.md Rule 1's input-domain tightening clause (the golden
  snapshot is byte-identical across it; conditions, tradeoffs, and alternatives: ADR-0002
  and ADR-0003).

## 10. Testable invariants

These must always hold and are tested in `tests/unit/` (T7):

1. **Determinism:** same seed + same config → byte-identical outputs. No randomness anywhere;
   all sorts use the defined tie-breakers.
2. **Bounds:** every component score and final score ∈ [0, 100]. (Proof sketch: MM maps into
   [0,100]; blended components are convex combinations of MM values; the final score is a convex
   combination of components.)
3. **Weight sum:** `weights` sum to exactly 1.0, as do all sub-blends and the accolade weights;
   peak-scope renormalized weights sum to exactly 1.0. The engine asserts and errors on
   violation — it never silently renormalizes. (Verification note: "exactly" is decimal intent;
   the engine checks the float64 sum at 1e-12 tolerance — a decimal-exact vector carries only
   ~1e-16 representation error, so anything beyond 1e-12 is real config drift, not rounding.
   Accolade weights must additionally be strictly positive, so no player's eligible-award mix
   can renormalize over a zero denominator in §5.5.)
4. **No NaN:** no NaN in any component or final output; nulls never become zeros (§9).
5. **Component monotonicity:** increasing a positively-oriented input of one player (holding all
   else fixed) never decreases that player's affected component score, and strictly increases it
   when the player is strictly inside the pool range for the affected sub-metric (min–max is
   weakly monotone at both boundaries: the pool max stays at 100, and a unique pool minimum
   moves with a rising min-holder, staying at 0). Tests assert strict increase on an
   interior fixture player. Global-score monotonicity is *not* claimed — min/max anchors can
   move other players' scores; that is expected pool-relative behavior (§12.2).
6. **Version tags:** every output row carries `method_version` (echoed verbatim from config) and
   `git_sha` when available.

## 11. Consensus sanity check (Spearman)

- Compute the **Spearman rank correlation** between the Default-Mode career ranking and one
  published consensus list (chosen and cited in T9, e.g., ESPN or The Ringer), over the
  intersection of players. Report the coefficient honestly in `docs/report.md`.
- Consensus is a **sanity check, never a target**. Do not chase the correlation toward 1.0.
- A large deviation is a smoke detector: investigate it. Usually it reveals a real method flaw
  (most often an era distortion or a mis-calibrated component) → fix the method. Occasionally it
  is a genuine, explainable, defensible difference → keep it and say why.
- **The iron rule, restated: if a result looks wrong, fix the method, not the player.** Players
  are never hand-placed into rankings.

## 12. Design decisions, assumptions, and limitations

The nine interrogation decisions, each documented with its reasoning, plus residual
limitations. Nothing here is silent; every entry is defensible in an interview.

### 12.1 Era baseline: league-mean ratios, not literal z-scores

Vision §3 words the objective layer as "era-relative z-scores." v1 deliberately substitutes
**league-mean ratio normalization** (`REL_s = per75_s / baseline_s`, §3). Why: true league
z-scores require a per-season league **standard deviation** for every metric, which can only be
computed by acquiring every qualified player-season league-wide back to 1957 — a data pull that
would blow up the committed-seed scope (15–30 players plus one small league reference table).
Ratios need only league means and pace, which fit in `data/seed/league_seasons.csv`. The intent
of the Vision wording — standardize against era context before combining — is fully preserved:
a ratio to the league mean *is* an era-relative standardization, and because min–max scaling is
invariant to affine transforms, inserting a pool-level z-score step before §6 would change no
score. Pool z-scores may still be reported as diagnostics.

### 12.2 Min–max makes every score pool-relative — a known property, not a bug

Every min–max-scaled **element** is anchored by **this specific 15–30 player pool**: the
best great anchors 100 and the weakest anchors 0 on each scaled element. The blended
components (Winning/Impact, Efficiency, Accolades) and the final score are convex
combinations of those elements — they inherit the pool-relativity but are **not
re-stretched** (§6), so they stay within [0, 100] without anchoring it (on the current
seed, career finals span roughly 16–69 and peak finals roughly 23–78; no player sits at
0 or 100). Scores are therefore
*relative standing among the seeded all-time greats*, not a fixed universal scale — a final
score of 57 means nothing outside this pool, and **expanding or shrinking the seed pool later
will shift everyone's numbers** (new min/max anchors) even though the method is unchanged. This
is stated up front so rank tables are read correctly. Rank order among unchanged players is far
more stable than raw scores, and the golden snapshots pin behavior to a fixed synthetic pool
precisely so pool changes in the real seed are never mistaken for method changes.

### 12.3 Winning/Impact 50/50 sub-weight: fixed in v1, reasoned, tunable later

The 50/50 split between `MM(WS48)` and `MM(SRS_w)` is a **fixed v1 sub-weight**. Reasoning:
neither signal is trustworthy alone — team SRS credits and blames players for their teammates
(Russell over-credited for Celtics depth, early Wilt punished for weak rosters), while Win
Shares is an individual estimate that leans on efficiency and box production. An equal split is
the honest prior: it lets the individual signal correct the team signal and vice versa, and any
unequal split would be a stronger claim than v1 has evidence for. It lives in config as an
objective-layer parameter and is explicitly tunable in a future version via the v2 path
(§8) — for example if a teammate-adjusted measure is added.

### 12.4 PER/BPM/VORP deferred to v2 — era-uniformity over richness

Vision §5 flavors the Efficiency/Advanced component with "PER, BPM, WS, VORP, and raw stats."
v1 deliberately uses none of PER/BPM/VORP: BPM and VORP exist only from 1973-74, PER is patchy
before 1974, and WS is already spoken for by Winning/Impact. Including them "where available"
would mean the component silently scores different eras on different inputs — **recreating the
exact era-distortion (method gap vs. genuine greatness) that questions.md Q2 exists to catch.**
Every player is instead scored on identical all-era inputs (REL_TS + SPI_career). PER/BPM/VORP
are deferred to a v2 enhancement with an explicit, documented era-handling design.

### 12.5 Peak N = 5 with a ≥50% games-played qualifier

A season enters the Peak window only if `AVAIL ≥ 0.5` — the player appeared in at least 50% of
his team's schedule. This stops low-minute, small-sample seasons (a 20-game injury-year hot
streak) from faking a peak season, while scaling with schedule length so lockout/COVID seasons
qualify normally. **N = 5** was chosen over the alternatives deliberately: N = 3 makes one
anomalous season a third of the window (too noisy); N = 7 blurs "peak" into prime-longevity and
double-counts what the Longevity component already measures. Five seasons is also the consensus
"prime" window in GOAT debates, which keeps the definition legible. `peak_n` is configurable as
an objective-layer parameter.

### 12.6 Playoff component excludes series wins and rings — by design

Playoff = production × depth (`Σ P_SPI × playoff_GP`) and nothing else. Playoff **series wins
were considered and excluded**: team playoff success already enters the index through Accolades
(rings, Finals MVP) and Winning/Impact (team SRS), so counting series wins here would
triple-count team success — the exact correlated-signal trap Vision §5 warns against ("don't
triple-count correlated signals"). Depth (games played) still enters, but as a *volume
multiplier on the player's own production*, not as a team-success credit: a long run only helps
a player who actually produced during it.

### 12.7 Accolades era-fairness — the Bill Russell worked example

This component is the direct answer to questions.md's era-fairness concern, and the fix is
demonstrable, not just asserted. Concretely:

Bill Russell's career spans seasons 1957–1969 (13 seasons, end-year convention). DPOY was
introduced in 1983.

- **Naive (rejected) treatment:** DPOY rate = 0 wins / 13 seasons = 0.0 → Russell, the most
  celebrated defender in NBA history, scores the pool minimum on the defensive award — punished
  for an award that did not exist.
- **v1 treatment:** DPOY-eligible seasons = 0 → DPOY is **excluded from his award mix
  entirely**, and his remaining award weights (MVP .30, ring .25, Finals MVP .15, All-NBA .15,
  All-Star .075) are renormalized by their sum (0.925). His accolade score is built only from
  awards he could actually win: MVP rate = 5/13, ring rate = 11/13 (a pool-max anchor), All-Star
  rate = 12/13, Finals MVP eligible only in his final season, 1969 (exact counts are verified
  against documented sources in T3).
- The same machinery handles every award asymmetry: no MVP before 1956, no Finals MVP before
  1969, no All-Star eligibility in 1999 (no game held). No player is credited for hypothetical
  wins either — eligibility bounds the denominator, never inflates the numerator.

### 12.8 Default weights: stated priorities as first-pass numbers — not learned, not optimized

The default weight vector (.25/.20/.18/.15/.12/.10) is **Roman's stated priority order from
Vision §5 translated into first-pass numeric weights**. It was not learned from data, not
optimized against any target, and not tuned toward consensus lists — doing any of those would
relocate subjectivity into a prediction-target choice (Vision §3) or violate the consensus
policy (§11). It is fully user-adjustable in Custom Mode: the `weights:` block in config is the
supported surface for disagreement. **Longevity is deliberately the smallest weight** because
career length is already partially rewarded elsewhere — Playoff depth accumulates over long
careers and per-eligible-season accolade rates give long careers more chances to sustain rate —
so weighting Longevity higher would double-reward the same underlying trait.

### 12.9 Peak scope = the full engine on a restricted window (one engine, many configurations)

Peak Mode is **not** a separate scoring path: it reuses the full scoring engine with each
player's input restricted to his top-5 qualifying seasons. Winning/Impact, Playoff, Efficiency,
and Accolades (season-attributed) are all recomputed over that window; Longevity is dropped and
its 0.10 weight is redistributed proportionally across the remaining five components
(`w_c / 0.90`, summing to exactly 1.0). **Peak Mode and Custom Mode are architecturally the
same mechanism — one engine, different configuration** (restricted input window in one case,
different weight vector in the other). That is the interview line, and it is literally true in
the implementation: there is exactly one scoring function, parameterized by config.

### 12.10 Residual limitations (accepted and documented)

1. League pace is used for all players (team-level pace exists from 1974 — v2 refinement
   candidate).
2. Playoff production is normalized against regular-season league baselines (league playoff
   baselines are small, noisy samples; the approximation affects all eras alike).
3. All-NBA 3rd team exists only from 1989, giving modern players extra 0.25-level selection
   chances in the All-NBA rate (small, documented asymmetry).
4. Steals, blocks, and turnovers are excluded from SPI for the same era-uniformity reason as
   §12.4 — v2 candidates with explicit era handling.
5. Active players (LeBron, Curry, Durant) are scored on careers "so far" as of the seed pull
   date recorded in `docs/sources.md` — Longevity and accolade rates are honest, not projected.
6. Pre-1978 pace values are hand-assembled, flagged `pace_estimated` in the league reference
   table (the possession estimate needs turnovers, tracked from 1977-78; pace for 1978+ is
   computed from the acquired league data). The 1974–77 values are Basketball-Reference's
   tracked league pace, verified; the 1957–73 values are unverified estimates — no published
   source containing them could be identified. The provenance investigation, an error bound
   from a reproducible cross-check, and the reasoning for retaining them are in
   `docs/sources.md`; replacing them with a formula-derived series is a v2 candidate.
7. Accolades must join a played player-season (rates in §5.5 divide by played eligible
   seasons; peak scope in §7 joins awards to window seasons). Exactly one real selection
   cannot: Magic Johnson retired before 1991-92 with zero games played, then was voted into
   and played the February 1992 All-Star Game. That selection is excluded from the seed
   (`ACCOLADE_EXCLUSIONS` in `pipeline/fetch_seed.py`), so Magic's All-Star rate is 11 in 13
   eligible seasons, not 12 — a fabricated zero-game 1992 season row was rejected because it
   would poison per-possession and availability calculations. The alternative was accepted as
   the honest, smaller distortion.

## 13. Hand-computability

A reader can compute any player's final score from this document given the seed inputs. That
claim is verified: `docs/methodology/v1_hand_worksheet.md` hand-computes a three-player
synthetic pool (full detail for PlayerA) through every formula above, and T7's golden snapshot
must reproduce those numbers within ±0.01. The worksheet's synthetic players are the core of the
valid fixture mini-set (T5); changing them after the golden snapshot exists is a version-bump
event.
